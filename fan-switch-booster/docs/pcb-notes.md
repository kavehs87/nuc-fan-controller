# PCB notes — 12 V fan driver + NUC fan-header interface + Arduino Pro Mini, single copper layer

The layout was generated by `pcb/build_board.py`, and the board in
`mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb` is that layout **stripped down to a routing
skeleton**: every footprint in its place, every pad carrying its net, no copper
tracks at all. The routing is yours to draw. Everything below is what the
generator produced and what `kicad-cli` verified on **2026-10-22**.

```
Board: 56.0 × 92.0 mm, one copper layer (B.Cu), zero vias, 30 footprints,
       0 track segments, one 0 ohm link (JP1) waiting to be wired

DRC on the skeleton:  Found 7 DRC violations      <- all cosmetic, see below
                      Found 68 unconnected items  <- expected: nothing is routed yet
        (rules: 0.50 mm clearance, 0.80 mm minimum track — the home-etch rules)
```

The 7 violations are all `lib_footprint_mismatch`: the generator writes its own
copy of a library footprint, and the copy differs from the library in ways that
do not matter electrically — the pad copper carries the baked placement rotation,
and library text below 0.8 mm is skipped because it cannot be etched. KiCad
notices; KiCad's copper does not.

| ref | footprint on the board | library it is compared against |
|---|---|---|
| MK1–MK4 | `MountingHole_3.2mm_M3` | `MountingHole` |
| Q4 | `TO-92_Inline_Wide` | `Package_TO_SOT_THT` |
| C4 | `CP_Radial_D6.3mm_P2.50mm` | `Capacitor_THT` |
| C5 | `CP_Radial_D8.0mm_P3.50mm` | `Capacitor_THT` |

`U1` is **not** in that list: the Pro Mini socket is generated by
`pcb/pro_mini.py` into the project's own footprint library, so board and library
agree byte for byte.

## How to rebuild everything

```bash
cd mosfet-fan-switch
python3 pcb/pro_mini.py            # the module's socket footprint and schematic symbol
python3 pcb/build_board.py         # the layout, WITH the generated routing
python3 tools/skeleton_board.py    # delete the copper tracks, keep the placement
kicad-cli pcb drc --output pcb/drc.txt --severity-all mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
```

`pcb/pro_mini.py` is the single source of truth for the module: the hole map, the
35-pad socket footprint and the `Arduino_Pro_Mini_328` schematic symbol all come
out of the same table, so a hole, a pad and a symbol pin are one number.

Two states of the same board are available:

| want | how |
|---|---|
| the **skeleton** now in the tree (footprints placed, pads netted, nothing routed) | what `tools/skeleton_board.py` produces from the generated board |
| the **generated routing** (64 tracks, with the defects listed below) | re-run `pcb/build_board.py`, or `git show a669df3:mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb` for the pre-Pro-Mini version |

```bash
kicad-cli pcb export gerbers --layers "B.Cu,B.Mask,B.SilkS,Edge.Cuts" --output pcb/gerber mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export drill --excellon-units mm --generate-map --map-format pdf --output pcb/drill mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export pdf --mode-single --output docs/pcb-etch-bottom.pdf \
        --layers "B.Cu,Edge.Cuts" --mirror --black-and-white --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export svg --mode-single --output docs/pcb-etch-bottom.svg \
        --layers "B.Cu,Edge.Cuts" --mirror --black-and-white --exclude-drawing-sheet \
        --page-size-mode 2 --fit-page-to-board --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
# the SMD parts are on the copper side, so the assembly drawing you build from is the bottom one
kicad-cli pcb export pdf --mode-single --output docs/pcb-assembly-bottom.pdf \
        --layers "B.Cu,B.Mask,B.SilkS,B.Fab,Edge.Cuts" --mirror --black-and-white \
        --sketch-pads-on-fab-layers --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export pdf --mode-single --output docs/pcb-assembly-top.pdf \
        --layers "F.SilkS,F.Fab,Edge.Cuts" --black-and-white --sketch-pads-on-fab-layers \
        --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb render --side top    --background opaque --width 1150 --height 1900 --output docs/pcb-render-top.png    mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb render --side bottom --background opaque --width 1150 --height 1900 --output docs/pcb-render-bottom.png mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb

The `.kicad_pcb` started life **generated** — `pcb/build_board.py` is where the part
table, the track list and the design rules live. The skeleton in the tree is that output
with the copper removed, so the placement and every pad's net are still the generator's
work; only the tracks are missing, and those are the part you are taking over. If the
board is open in KiCad while a script rewrites the file, KiCad will say it changed on
disk: choose **File → Revert** rather than saving, or your editor session overwrites
everything the script wrote.

## Design rules used

Set in `mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pro` (netclass *Default*), chosen for a home-etched
board in ~35 µm copper:

| rule | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| clearance (different nets) | **0.50 mm** | what a toner-transfer / home-UV board reliably holds; the DRC is run against this |
| minimum track width | **0.80 mm** | survives etchant under-attack; also the widest thing a hobby cutter likes |
| signal tracks | **0.80 mm** | gate and logic nodes, including the NUC band and everything on the module deck |
| `/GND` bus along the top edge | **2.00 mm** | the return for everything |
| `/GND` return lane down the left edge | **1.50 mm** | brings the return back to J1, C3 and C4 |
| `/GND` descent + bus through the NUC band | **1.50 mm** | the band's and the module's own return, fed past the end of the 12 V bus |
| `/P12V` bus | **3.00 mm** | the supply rail, ~1 A with margin |
| `/DRAIN` runs to J3 and D1 | **1.50 mm** | 300 mA of fan current plus the switching edges |
| minimum drill | **0.80 mm** | standard hobby bit |
| vias | **none** | single-layer board |

The generator checks every net pair for crossings and prints
`no net crossings - the board is planar, no jumpers needed` — which is true **of the
tracks it generates**. It is not the whole story: the one net it deliberately does not
generate, `/P12V` → U1 pad 13, is the interesting one, and it is discussed under
*The one connection the generator will not draw*.

## Where things sit

Board origin is the top-left corner of the outline, X right, **Y down** (KiCad's board
convention). The board is **56 × 92 mm**: the fan driver occupies the top 56 × 44 exactly
as in the first version, the NUC interface band runs from y = 42 to y = 59, and the
**module deck** — where the Arduino now lives — fills y = 60 to 92. Four 3.2 mm mounting
holes at the corners are what set the size.

```
      x=0                                                     x=56
 y=3  ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════   /GND bus, 2.0 mm wide
      R2  R4  R5        MK2                     MK1     ┊ D2 (spare diode)
                        Q2  →  Q3  →  Q1   →  J3 (3 fans)
  y=41 ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════   /P12V bus, 3.0 mm wide
      J1 (+12 V in)   C3 C4  R3   D1                    ┊ D3 (spare diode)
 ───────────────────────────── the NUC band ──────────────────────
      C6 C5 R8        J6 (ICSP)      R7   R9  J4 (NUC header)
                     Q4
  y=58 ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════   /GND band rail, 1.5 mm
 ───────────────────────────── the module deck ───────────────────
      ┊ /TACH_PWM down x=3.4                    J5 (FTDI header)
      ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐   1 DTR … 6 BLK
      │  U1  Arduino Pro Mini 3V3, on sockets│
      └──────────────────────────────────────┘
  y=89.5  MK3                                    MK4

A 1.5 mm `/GND` return lane runs down the left edge (x = 5.5) from the top bus to J1, C3
and C4, so no current has to travel the whole length of the board twice. The band and the
module get their return the other way: the top bus runs right to x = 51.5, drops down the
right edge **past the end of the 12 V bus** all the way to y = 75.57, and turns left into
U1's programming-row GND pad. The band itself is fed from that same descent at y = 58.

## Why the generated routing was stripped

The "DRC clean" claim in an earlier version of this file came from a stale report.
`kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all` on the routed board (commit `a669df3`) reported
**8 violations and 2 unconnected pads**:

| what | where | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **JP1 was placed but never wired** | the `/PWM` corridor track ended 3.8 mm from pad 1 | 2 unconnected pads + a dangling 30 mm track. The jumper is the whole point of the single-layer crossing, and it was decorative |
| **JP1 pad 1 sat on the `/GND` return lane** | the lane runs down x=5.5 | `shorting_items` `/PWM` vs `/GND`, plus a `solder_mask_bridge` |
| **the top-right mounting hole sat on the `/GND` band** | hole (53.5,2.5) Ø3.2 mm; the band drops down x=51.5 at 1.5 mm width | 2 `hole_clearance` errors — the band's copper reaches x=52.25, the hole's edge is at 51.9 |
| **`/TBASE` came too close to the band rail** | `/TBASE` (43,51.62)→(43,57); the `/GND` rail runs along y=58 | 1 `shorting_items` |
| **the band rail passed too close to Q4** | rail at y=58 vs Q4 pad 2 `/TBASE` (43,57) and pad 3 `/NUC_TACH` (45.54,57) | 2 `solder_mask_bridge` — on a home-etched board a thin mask bridge is where etch undercut starts |

JP1's pads have since been moved to (2.00, 14) and (9.62, 14) so the two pads genuinely
straddle the GND spine at x = 5.5 instead of sitting on it. The other three are
**keep-outs, not fixed defects**: the copper is gone, so the skeleton passes, and they
become your responsibility the moment you draw a track.

### Keep-outs to remember while you route

- **Mounting holes are Ø3.2 mm.** Keep copper ≥1.85 mm from a hole centre (1.6 mm radius
  + 0.25 mm hole clearance) — **≥2.6 mm from the centre line for a 1.5 mm wide band**.
  The top-right hole at (53.5,2.5) against a band at x=51.5 is exactly that violation.
- **Wide buses have round ends.** A 2.0 mm bus bulges 1.0 mm past its endpoint, which is
  how the `/GND` bus turning at (51.5,3) reached into the mounting hole.
- **The band rail along y=58 is the busiest keep-out in the band.** Q4's pads sit at
  y=57 on a 2.54 mm pitch, so a 1.5 mm rail at y=58 leaves about 0.3 mm of mask between
  copper features. Run that rail lower (y ≥ 60), or feed Q4 from the other side.
- **JP1 has to actually bridge something.** Its pads are 7.62 mm apart at (2.00,14) and
  (9.62,14), and the `/GND` spine at x=5.5 passes exactly between them. `/PWM` comes up
  the 0.8 mm corridor at x=2.0, crosses on JP1, and continues at x=14 to R1. If you route
  `/PWM` around the buses instead, JP1 can go away and the left-edge corridor is free.
- **The two full-width rails are walls.** `/P12V` along y=41 and the `/GND` band rail
  along y=58 (plus the descent at x=51.5) divide the board into three zones. Everything
  on the module deck has to reach its net through a gap at the ends of those rails, and
  there are only two: the **left edge** (x ≤ 4.8 is clear from y=44 down to the bottom
  bus) and the **right edge** (x ≥ 52 below D3's courtyard, but only if the `/GND`
  descent does not run down there as well).

### The corridor plan the generated feeds use

| net | how the generated routing gets it where it is going |
|---|---|
| `/PWM` | U1 pad 12 (14,87) → left to (2,87) → up the left edge to (2,14) → **JP1** across the GND spine → (14,14) → down to R1 pad 1 |
| `/TACH_PWM` | R9 pad 1 (40.5,44) → left along y=44 to (3.4,44) → down the left edge to (3.4,71.76) → right to U1 pad 24 |
| `/DEMAND` | the band rail along y=49 from x=30.08 to x=4.8 → down to (4.8,70) → one diagonal to U1 pad 20 at (24.16,71.76) |
| `/GND` (module) | the descent at x=51.5 from y=3 down to y=75.57 → left into U1 pad 29 |
| `/P12V` (module) | **not generated** — see below |

`/TACH_PWM` and `/DEMAND` both use the left edge, at x=3.4 and x=4.8. Together with the
`/GND` spine at x=5.5 and the `/PWM` corridor at x=2.0, that is **four parallel lanes in
6 mm of board edge** — 0.8 mm copper with 0.7 mm gaps. It fits the 0.5 mm clearance rule
with 0.2 mm to spare, which is the tightest place on the board. If you would rather not
thread four tracks there, the DEMAND drop is the one to relocate: it can come off the
band rail anywhere between x=8 and x=30 and drop down the middle of the module deck,
because the deck is empty except under the module.

### The one connection the generator will not draw

**`/P12V` → U1 pad 13 (RAW) at (41.94, 71.76) has no generated track**, and no planar
route exists with the rails where they are: pad 13 sits below both the `/P12V` bus
(y=41) and the band rail (y=58), and the `/GND` descent at x=51.5 runs down to y=75.57,
blocking the right-hand gap. Three honest ways to finish it:

1. **A second jumper**, exactly like JP1: two pads on either side of the band rail, a
   0 Ω link or a piece of tinned wire across it. This is what JP1 demonstrates.
2. **Move the module's GND feed.** Feed U1 pad 29 from the band rail instead (a short
   stub down from (44.48,58)) and stop the right-edge descent at y=58. That opens the
   whole right corridor — down x≈53, under D3's courtyard, to y≈71 — and `/P12V` to
   pad 13 needs no jumper at all. This is the version I would route.
3. **Feed RAW somewhere else.** The module's RAW pad is its regulator input; you could
   instead bring 12 V to the module through its own power pins and keep `/P12V` off the
   deck entirely. That changes the schematic, not just the routing.

Whatever you choose, **do not leave pad 13 unconnected**: it is how the module gets
powered. The 3.3 V on pads 16 and 28 is the module's *output*, not a rail this board
drives.

> in `pcb/pro_mini.py` is the **clone** layout (Geeetech / Baite / HiLetgo boards — what

> the SparkFun one, edit `ROW_SPACING`, `PADS_PER_ROW` and `SPARE` at the top of
> `pcb/pro_mini.py` and re-run the generator — do not force a SparkFun module into
> clone sockets.

12 V fan goes between +12 V and `/DRAIN`; the fan's own wire pair plugs straight into
one row. (`/FAN_W` in the schematic is inside the fan *model* — it never appears on
the board.)

## Programming the module

Two ways to get code into the ATmega328P, both on the board, both usable at the same
time (you just cannot use them simultaneously).

### J5 — an FTDI or CH340 breakout

J5 repeats the module's own programming row, pin for pin: **1 DTR, 2 TX0, 3 RX1,
4 VCC(3.3 V), 5 GND, 6 BLK**. Plug the adapter with its **black wire over the BLK end** —
that is the DTR line that resets the board and starts the bootloader.

- **Use a 3.3 V adapter.** A 5 V FTDI breakout puts 5 V on the module's RX line. The
  clone boards in the drawer are 3.3 V / 8 MHz parts; their ATmega328P RX pin tolerates
  5 V (it is not 5 V-tolerant on the output side, which is why TX0 into the adapter is
  fine), but there is no reason to test that theory on a board you intend to keep.
- Six 4.02 mm tracks between J5 and U1's programming row. Nothing else about this header
  is clever.
- `/P3V3` at J5 pad 4 is the **module's own regulator output**, not a rail this board
  drives. It powers the adapter's level shifter, and it is what the `/DEMAND` divider is
  referenced to.

### J6 — another Arduino as the programmer (Uno as ISP)

J6 is a standard 2×03 ICSP header: **1 MISO, 3 SCK, 4 MOSI, 5 RST, 6 GND**, and
**pin 2 is deliberately a dead pad**.

> **Five wires, not six.** Pin 2 of an ICSP header is the programmer's 5 V. An Uno
> programming a **3.3 V** Pro Mini must not push its 5 V into the target's VCC — the
> two boards would fight over the supply and the 3.3 V part gets a 5 V rail. So the
> Uno-as-ISP wiring is: Uno 10→J6-1 (MISO), 11→J6-3 (SCK), 12→J6-4 (MOSI), 9→J6-5 (RST),
> GND→J6-6, **and nothing to J6-2**. The Pro Mini powers itself from `/P12V` on its RAW
> pad while it is being programmed.

The ICSP runs cross under the module: J6 sits at x 14–20, y 60–69 while U1's SCK/MISO/
MOSI pads are at y 71.76 and its RST and GND are further right. Tracks passing **under
the module** are normal and fine — the module stands on its sockets, several mm above the
board — but plan the corridor before you commit, because the deck below U1 is the only
space available for `/RST` (pad 15) to reach J6 pin 5.

### In the IDE

Board: **Arduino Pro or Pro Mini**. Processor: **ATmega328P (3.3 V, 8 MHz)** — the 5 V /
16 MHz entry programs the wrong bootloader and the wrong fuse bits for this module.
Processor selection is what sets the bootloader's baud rate; pick wrong and the IDE
reports "programmer is not responding" while the wiring is perfect.

The two signals this board actually uses are **D9 = OC1A** (Timer 1 channel A) for
`/PWM`, and **D10 = OC1B** (Timer 1 channel B) for `/TACH_PWM`. Both are on the same
timer, which is convenient: one `TCCR1A`/`TCCR1B` setup drives the fan and the tach
carrier. A0 reads `/DEMAND`. Everything else on the module is spare.

## Which way round do Q2 and Q3 go? (they are mirror images — deliberately)

Because both SMD parts live on the copper side, their outlines and their `Q2` / `Q3`
labels are stored mirrored. Any **top-side** view of the board therefore shows them
reversed: `Q3` reads as `ƧQ` and pads 1–4 run right-to-left, so nothing in that view
tells you which pad is pin 1. That is how KiCad draws every bottom-side part — but it
reads like a mistake, so here is the check.

Hold the board with the **copper facing you**. Everything you knew from the top view is
now left–right reversed: the +12 V bus still runs along y = 41, but J3 (the fans) is on
your left and J1 (the input) on your right. That is what
`docs/pcb-assembly-bottom.pdf` and `docs/pcb-render-smd-copper.png` show:

```
       Q3 — IRF7416, SO-8, as seen from the copper side

             8     7     6     5      ← pads 5-8 = /HIGATE (drains)
         ┌───────────────────────────┐
         │                           │
      ▸  │  1     2     3     4      ← the notch ▸ sits beside pad 1
         └───────────────────────────┘
            └── 1-3 = /P12V ──┘  └ 4 = /BOOT (gate)
```

Numbering runs counter-clockwise around the body and the notch is next to pin 1 — the
same relationship the chip has in your hand. `Q2` (SOT-23) in the same view: pad 3
alone on the left (`/BOOT`, drain), pad 1 (`/LGATE`, gate) and pad 2 (`/GND`, source)
on the right, with the small silk triangle marking the pad-1 end.

**Against the datasheet.** The IRF7416 datasheet draws the SO-8 in *top view*: S, S, S,
G down the left edge (1–4) and D, D, D, D up the right edge (5–8, pin 5 at the bottom).
Turn that picture **90° counter-clockwise** and it is the board above: the left edge
becomes the near row with pin 1 at the left, the right edge becomes the far row with
pin 5 at the right. Nothing is mirrored; the chip is simply rotated, because on a
one-layer board it lies on the copper rather than standing on the component side.

**See it yourself in KiCad.** The footprints carry their library 3D models, so the
3D viewer (`View → 3D Viewer`, the teapot icon) shows a real SO-8 and SOT-23 sitting on
the copper. In the PCB editor, `View → Flip Board View` (and `Ctrl+H` cycles the display
modes) shows the whole board as if you were looking at it from the bottom — which is the
view you will actually have while soldering, and the point where `Q3` stops reading
backwards.

Two things show the copper is right, not just the picture:

- The footprint in `mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb` is **identical, coordinate for
  coordinate, to what KiCad itself writes** when you place
  `Package_SO:SO-8_3.9x4.9mm_P1.27mm` on B.Cu at rotation 90° — all 20 courtyard,
  silk and fab items match, including the notch polygon at local (−2.6, 2.47), which
  lands at board (32.97, 23.60), immediately beside pad 1 at (32.405, 23.575).
- The nets land on the real package pins: pads 1–3 → `/P12V`, pad 4 → `/BOOT`, pads
  5–8 → `/HIGATE`. That is a P-channel device with its sources on +12 V, its gate on
  the bootstrap node and its drains feeding the high-side gate node.

**Finding pin 1 without silkscreen.** You are etching copper only, so the notch may
never appear on the finished board. The nets settle it anyway: pads 1, 2 and 3 are all
`/P12V` and pad 4 is `/BOOT`, so whatever routing you draw, *the row whose three pads end
up merged into the fat +12 V bus is the source row, and the odd one out in that row is pin
4, the gate*. A multimeter confirms it before soldering — pads 1–3 continuous with +12 V,
pad 4 not. If you would rather have the marker physically there, plot
`--layers "B.Cu,B.SilkS"` for the transfer instead of `B.Cu` alone; the 0.2 mm silk
lines are thin toner and often rub off, which is why the assembly PDF is the reliable
route.

## Etching this board

1. Print `docs/pcb-etch-bottom.svg` (or the PDF) at **100 % — do not scale to fit**.
   The SVG's page is exactly the board size, which makes the check trivial.
2. The artwork is already **mirrored**: it is the bottom (copper) side as seen from
   the top. Put the printed side **down** onto the copper, exactly as you would for
   toner transfer. If you print the PDF on A4, line the outline up with the board
   edge before you cut.
3. Black areas are copper that stays. Everything white gets etched away.
4. Cut the outline from `Edge.Cuts` (the rounded rectangle in the plot). The four
   corner holes are on the outline's radius, so drill them before or after etching —
   not through the toner.
5. Drill, then tin the pads. The solder mask layer (`B_Mask.gbs`) is exported only so
   a fab would have it; for a home board it is the drill-and-tin guide.

Silkscreen: the through-hole labels live on **F.SilkS**, the component side. Since
there is no copper on that side the labels never sit on copper, and they read normally
when the board is face-up. `B.SilkS` carries the two SMD reference labels plus their
notch markers, and reads normally only when the **copper** faces you. If you transfer
copper only, use `docs/pcb-assembly-bottom.pdf` as the assembly sheet for Q2 and Q3 —
it is the copper side, with every pad outlined and numbered.

The board prints these notes on the component side: `12V FAN DRIVER · 3 x 100 mA`,
`+12V IN`, `FAN OUT`, `Q1 G-D-S`, `D1 K +12V`, `D2 D3 spare`, `JP1 = 0 ohm link`,
`NUC INTERFACE`, `NUC FAN HEADER`, `PRO MINI 328 SOCKET`, `J5: 1=DTR 6=BLK`,
`ICSP: no 5V wire`, `copper on back`.

## Assembly order

1. **Q2 (SOT-23) and Q3 (SO-8)** — on the **copper** side, and mirror images of the
   library drawings because of it: read *Which way round do Q2 and Q3 go?* first.
   Solder and inspect these before anything else; everything depends on them. Check Q3
   with a multimeter before continuing: pads 1–3 (source) must be continuous with the
   +12 V bus, pad 4 must be isolated from it.
2. **R1–R5, R7–R9** — values matter here: R2 and R5 are what keep the fans from
   drifting on at power-up. R7/R8 must be the same value or the divider no longer sits
   at half the NUC's rail.
3. **D1** — the banded end goes to the +12 V side. Backwards, it is a dead short
   across the supply through the FET's body diode. **Leave D2 and D3 empty.**
4. **C4 and C5** — `+` to pad 1. C3 and C6 are not polarised.
5. **Q4 (TO-92)** — flat face towards you, leads down: left = emitter to `/GND`,
   middle = base to `/TBASE`, right = collector to `/NUC_TACH`. See the warning under
   *Semiconductors*; backwards, the NUC reads a stalled fan.
6. **JP1** — the 0 Ω link across the GND return spine at (2.00, 14) → (9.62, 14). Fit
   it before the tall parts get in the way. A 0 Ω axial with the leads spread, or a
   piece of tinned wire soldered between the two pads.
7. **The three female socket strips for U1** — solder them with the module *not* in
   place, checked square against the silkscreen and the hole map. If you have measured
   your module and it is the SparkFun layout, stop here and regenerate the footprint.
8. **J5 and J6** — the programming headers. Short, and they make the deck rigid.
9. **Q1 (TO-220)** — the tab is the **drain** on this part as mounted, and the tab is
   live at 12 V switching. Keep it clear of anything on the top side; if you bolt it
   to a heatsink, the heatsink is live too.
10. **J1, J3, J4** last, so the board lies flat while you solder.
11. Mount, then wire: 12 V brick → J1, three fans → J3, the NUC's fan cable → J4,
    the Pro Mini → its sockets. The Arduino, the 12 V supply and the NUC must all
    share the same ground.
12. **Program the module before fitting it** if you can: with the module out of the
    sockets, J5 or J6 are reachable from the top and the FTDI cable lies flat. With the
    module in place, program through J5 as normal.

## The NUC link

What the band between the 12 V bus and the module deck does, and the numbers that say
it works (`sim/nuc_link.py`, run by `./sim/run.sh`):

- **Reading the request.** The NUC drives its fan header's PWM line at 5 V
  (≈ 23 kHz on modern ITEs). `R7` (10 k, in series, at the header) and `R8` (10 k, to
  ground) halve it: **2.50 V at 100 % duty = 775 counts** of a 3.3 V ADC reference,
  0.80 V of headroom. `C5` (1 µ) + `C6` (100 n) at the tap give a measured
  **5.49 ms** time constant — 126 PWM periods — so the ADC reads the mean, not the
  square wave; residual ripple is **≤ 5 mV** at every duty. The divider draws
  **250 µA** from the NUC's PWM output at full duty, which is nothing to an ITE.
- **Answering on the tach line.** `Q4` is an open collector: `/TACH_PWM` (the Pro
  Mini's D10 / OC1B) drives its base through `R9` (1 k) — 2.58 mA of base drive
  sinking 4.96 mA of the NUC's pull-up current, so β only has to reach 1.9 and a
  BC547 gives 200+. The line swings **5.00 V high / 0.037 V low** (Vce(sat) 37 mV,
  with a 202 mV switching kick). There is no pull-up resistor on the board: the NUC
  carries its own, which is why `V4` + `R10` are DNP stand-ins on the sheet.
- **The carrier frequency is not free.** An ITE counts **two pulses per revolution**,
  so `RPM = 30 × f_tach`. The legal window — 700 to 10 500 RPM — is **23 to 350 Hz**
  on this wire. The fan drive itself runs at 1 kHz, which would read 30 000 RPM and be
  rejected: that is why the tach branch has its own carrier (100 Hz here, reading
  3 000 RPM) instead of copying the PWM that drives the fans.
- **J4 pin 3 is not connected.** That pin is the NUC's own fan supply; this board
  powers the fans from its own 12 V brick and must not be asked to source or sink it.

## KiCad 10 behaviour that shaped this generator

Kept here because it cost real time to find, and it will bite the next board:

- **A footprint's rotation rotates pad *positions* only; a pad's copper rotates only
  by that pad's own `(at x y angle)`.** KiCad itself bakes the placement angle into
  every pad when it places a footprint (a fresh SO-8 at `(at 10 10 90)` is saved with
  pads written `(at -2.575 -1.905 90)`). A hand-written footprint with pad angles left
  at 0 gives you an SO-8 whose pads are still 1.75 × 0.6 mm in an unrotated row —
  overlapping copper, and DRC shorts that look like routing mistakes.
  `build_board.py` bakes the angle: `la = (la - c.a) % 360`.
- **Track end caps are round.** A 2.0 mm track reaching a point bulges 1.0 mm past it.
  That is how a +12 V bus running along an SO-8's pad row shorted into the gate pad
  1.27 mm away. Keep wide runs off dense SMD rows.
- **Text angles in the file are absolute (board-relative)**, not relative to the
  footprint. Emitting `(at x y 0)` gives a label that is horizontal on the board no
  matter how the footprint is rotated; emitting the footprint's own angle gives you a
  label sideways. Board `gr_text` is also **centred** on its position, so a note
  placed at x=29 really starts at x≈25.
- **KiCad mirrors a B.Cu footprint by negating the pad's local Y** — the emitter does
  the same, and writes the footprint angle as `-c.a` because KiCad's rotation
  handedness is the negative of the script's maths convention. The mirrored silk and
  label that come out of it are correct for the copper side and look wrong from the
  top; see *Which way round do Q2 and Q3 go?*
- **Plotting `B.*` layers is drawn in top-side coordinates**, so bottom-side silk reads
  backwards unless you add `--mirror`. The first `pcb-assembly-top.pdf` plotted
  `F.SilkS,F.Fab,B.Cu,B.SilkS` unmirrored, which is what made the IRF7416 look mounted
  from the wrong side of the board. The assembly drawings are now split: bottom-side
  artwork mirrored, top-side artwork `F.*` only.
- **`kicad-cli pcb export svg` draws the board's drawing sheet** — frame, grid, `Rev:
  v2`, date — *inside* the board area unless you pass `--exclude-drawing-sheet`. The
  first `pcb-etch-bottom.svg` carried all of it (75 KB instead of 15 KB). `export pdf`
  has no such flag: it leaves the border and title out unless you ask for them with
  `--include-border-title`.
- Footprint library texts smaller than 0.8 mm are skipped by the generator: they are
  too small to etch, and they were the source of the `text_thickness` / `text_height`
  violations.
- The board's footprints reuse the **schematic symbols' UUIDs**, so cross-probing
  between the sheet and the board works and *Update PCB from Schematic* recognises
  the parts instead of treating the board as 30 unknown footprints. The four mounting
  holes have their own UUIDs; they have no symbol, which is normal. Re-run
  `pcb/build_board.py` after any schematic change and the board follows the sheet.
- **KiCad's schematic editor silently throws away junction dots** when a script adds
  or deletes a component, a wire, or a label — even `validate_schematic`'s save does
  it. The ERC then jumps to a dozen `unconnected_wire_endpoint` /
  `pin_not_connected` / `power_pin_not_driven` violations that look like the edit
  broke the wiring. It did not: the copper of the wires is still there, only the dots
  are gone. `python3 tools/fix_schematic_junctions.py --rebuild
  mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_sch` recomputes every junction from the wires and puts them
  back; run it after any scripted schematic edit, before the ERC. `--check` reports
  without writing.
- **`kicad-cli` resolves a symbol from the sheet's own `lib_symbols` cache, not from
  the library.** A script that inserts an instance whose `lib_id` has no cached
  definition loads, but ERC sees a symbol with no pins: it reports `Pin not connected`
  **at the instance's anchor**, `Label not connected` for every net label on it, and
  prints the pin as `Pin 1 [???, ?]`. The fix is what KiCad itself would have written
  on save: copy the library's `(symbol "Name" …)` block into the cache, renamed to
  `(symbol "Library:Name" …)`. `tools/embed_pro_mini_schematic.py` does this.
- **A `.kicad_sch` wire is exactly two points.** A polyline with three points in one
  `(wire (pts …))` block makes `kicad-cli` refuse the whole file with
  `Failed to load schematic` and no other clue. Draw an L as two wires.
- **ERC's connection grid is 1.27 mm.** `endpoint_off_grid` fires on a pin or wire end
  that is not a multiple of it. Symbol pin offsets are multiples of 1.27, so choosing
  anchors on the grid (62.23, 158.75 rather than 62, 158) makes the whole batch of
  warnings disappear.
- **`kicad-cli` needs the project context next to the schematic.** Copy a `.kicad_sch`
  to a scratch directory and every command answers `Failed to load schematic`, because
  `sym-lib-table`, `fp-lib-table` and the `.kicad_pro` are not there. This looks
  exactly like a corrupt file and sent a bisect down a dead end: test in the project
  directory, or copy those three files along with it.
- **KiCad draws a rotated net label *from* its anchor**, so four vertical `/HIGATE`
  labels attached to a symbol's bottom row each ran 6.6 mm upward, straight through the
  symbol body. Tie the legs with a wire and label the node once. For a horizontal label
  the anchor sits at the named edge: `justify left` at angle 0 puts the text to the
  right of the anchor, `justify right` at angle 180 puts it to the left — which is why
  a label on a left-hand pin (whose graphic points right, into the body) is written
  `angle 180 justify right`.
- **A pin whose endpoint lands in the interior of a wire is not connected.** ERC calls it
  `Pin not connected` and the netlist calls that terminal `unconnected-_Q3-D-Pad7_`.
  Wires that tie several legs together have to be **segmented** so each pin lands on a
  segment endpoint: a single long wire across four drain legs connected the two at the
  ends and the one with its own junction, and silently left the fourth adrift.
- **A net label attached straight to a pin endpoint, with no wire, is a connection.**
  ERC is clean and the netlist carries the net. That is how all 35 of U1's holes are
  wired on the sheet: 18 labels, 17 no-connects, one wire to the GND rail.
- **`Simulation_SPICE:NPN` cannot be used as a switch on a board that also carries
  PWR_FLAG symbols**: KiCad's ERC reports `pin_to_pin` between its collector and
  emitter through the power flags, and the part is a pure SPICE symbol with no
  physical identity. That is why Q4 became a real `Transistor_BJT`-style symbol.
- **KiCad 10 ignores `Sim.Pins` for `.model` cards.** The mapping field is accepted,
  silently unused: every device line is written in **symbol pin order**. So the pin
  numbers *are* the SPICE terminal order, and the only way to get ngspice's order
  (MOSFET `D G S B`, BJT `C B E`) is to make the symbol's pin order the package order
  and re-order the terminals in a `.subckt` wrapper. `Sim.Pins` on a symbol that
  exports as `M`/`Q`/`D` does nothing at all.
- **ngspice wants four nodes for a MOSFET card.** `M d g s model` is rejected with
  `not enough nodes`; `M d g s s model` (bulk spelled out, tied to the source) runs.
  KiCad never noticed, because its own 4-pin substrate symbols always supplied four.
- **Sub-circuit calls are honoured by the SPICE exporter.** `Sim.Library` + `Sim.Name`
  with **no** `Sim.Type` makes KiCad write `XQ1 … mos_irfz44n`, and the wrapper carries
  the terminal order. Keep the `.model` card in the same `.lib` file as its wrapper.
- **`.kicad_sym` files are version-strict.** A `(version 20260206)` stamp or a top-level
  `(comment "…")` block makes KiCad report `Unable to load library` and every symbol on
  the sheet turns into an unknown symbol. `(version 20251024)` with no comment block
  loads.
- **KiCad 10's `sym-lib-table` lists `(lib …)` entries directly under the header** — no
  `(libs …)` wrapper. With the wrapper the file parses, the library is simply not
  registered, and the ERC says `The current configuration does not include the symbol
  library '…'` for every instance.
- **KiCad's SPICE model reader rejects a `.model` card that continues on a `+`
  line** (`expected newline`). `sim/models/bc547.lib` has to stay one long line.

## Known limits of this layout

- It is a **learning board**, not a fab submission: 0.5 mm clearance and 0.8 mm tracks
  are generous for a modern fab and tight for a home etch. It is a good middle ground
  for both.
- No ground plane and no ground pours — the GND is a 2.0 mm bus along the top edge, a
  1.5 mm return lane down the left edge, a 1.5 mm descent down the right edge and a
  1.5 mm bus through the NUC band. For three 100 mA fans at 1 kHz that is fine; it is
  not what you would do for a 10 A output.
- **The board in the tree is unrouted.** 30 footprints sit where the generator put them,
  every pad carries its net, and there is no copper. The ratsnest is your guide; the
  generated routing exists in git (`a669df3` for the pre-module version) if you want to
  see one answer, defects and all.
- **`/P12V` → U1 pad 13 is not routed by the generator**, and no planar route exists with
  the rails where they are. Decide between a second jumper and re-homing the module's
  GND feed — see *The one connection the generator will not draw*. Leaving it open means
  the module never powers up.
- **JP1 is a real component that is not in the schematic.** It is meant to bridge the GND
  return lane so `/PWM` can get from the module deck back up to R1 without a via. Either
  wire it (two short tracks into its pads) or drop it and route `/PWM` around the buses —
  but decide, do not leave it floating. If you drop it, take it out of the BOM too.
- The board is **56 × 92 mm**, 35 % bigger than the version that assumed the Arduino was
  a box outside the outline. The module deck is what the extra 24 mm bought; the fan
  driver's placement is byte-identical to the first version (tag
  `checkpoint-fan-driver-v1`).
- **The hole map is the clone layout.** 15.24 mm row spacing, 12 pads per row. A genuine
  SparkFun Pro Mini is 12.70 mm and 13 pads. Measure before soldering; `pcb/pro_mini.py`
  is parameterised for exactly this argument.
- The module sits on **female headers**, so it stays swappable and programmable and can
  be replaced by an Uno-shaped board later. It also means the module's own components
  (its regulator, its crystal) hang above the board: keep nothing tall in the courtyard.
- Q1's TO-220 tab is the drain and is switching. Leave clearance around it if you
  ever route on the top side.
- The fan connector is a 2×03 header, not a screw terminal — that is what "use generic
  pin headers for the fan and 12 V connections" bought. If the fans will be tugged on,
  use a screw terminal or lock the cables to the enclosure instead.
- **The tach carrier has to stay between 23 and 350 Hz** (RPM = 30 × f, ITE window
  700–10 500 RPM). Nothing on the board enforces that: it is a firmware rule. Driving
  the tach line at the fan PWM's 1 kHz makes the NUC report a fan that is not there.
- The divider is 10 k / 10 k, tuned to a 5 V NUC request. If your board is ever asked
  to read a 3.3 V request instead, R7/R8 should be replaced by a 10 k / 20 k divider
  (or removed) rather than trusting the 2.50 V numbers here. Some boards drive a fan's
  PWM output at 12 V — meter the NUC's PWM line before trusting 5 V, because 12 V on
  that divider puts 6 V on a 3.3 V ADC pin.
- **D2 and D3 are unpopulated.** Footprints without parts are not defects, but they are
  copper that can short something if you route carelessly between them: D2's courtyard is
  6 mm wide against the right edge of the board, and D3's TO-220 pads sit 0.4 mm from the
  outline.
