# PCB notes — 12 V fan driver, single copper layer

The board is generated by `pcb/build_board.py`. Everything below is what that
script produced and what `kicad-cli` verified on **2026-10-22**.

```
DRC:  Found 0 violations
      Found 0 unconnected items
      (rules: 0.50 mm clearance, 0.80 mm minimum track — the home-etch rules)
Board: 56.0 × 44.0 mm, one copper layer (B.Cu), zero vias, 18 footprints, 42 track segments
```

## How to rebuild everything

```bash
cd mosfet-fan-switch
python3 pcb/build_board.py                                    # writes the .kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb drc --output pcb/drc.txt --severity-all mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb

kicad-cli pcb export gerbers --layers "B.Cu,B.Mask,B.SilkS,Edge.Cuts" --output pcb/gerber mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export drill --excellon-units mm --generate-map --map-format pdf --output pcb/drill mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export pdf --mode-single --output docs/pcb-etch-bottom.pdf \
        --layers "B.Cu,Edge.Cuts" --mirror --black-and-white --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export svg --mode-single --output docs/pcb-etch-bottom.svg \
        --layers "B.Cu,Edge.Cuts" --mirror --black-and-white --page-size-mode 2 --fit-page-to-board --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb export pdf --mode-single --output docs/pcb-assembly-top.pdf \
        --layers "F.SilkS,F.Fab,B.Cu,B.SilkS,Edge.Cuts" --black-and-white --scale 1 mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb render --side top    --width 1400 --height 1100 --output docs/pcb-render-top.png    mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb render --side bottom --width 1400 --height 1100 --output docs/pcb-render-bottom.png mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pcb
```

## Design rules used

Set in `mosfet-fan-switch.kicad_pro` (netclass *Default*), chosen for a home-etched
board in ~35 µm copper:

| rule | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| clearance (different nets) | **0.50 mm** | what a toner-transfer / home-UV board reliably holds; the DRC is run against this |
| minimum track width | **0.80 mm** | survives etchant under-attack; also the widest thing a hobby cutter likes |
| signal tracks | **0.80 mm** (34 segments) | gate and logic nodes |
| `/GND` bus along the top edge | **2.00 mm** | the return for everything |
| `/GND` return lane down the left edge | **1.50 mm** | brings the return back to J1, C3 and C4 |
| `/P12V` bus along the bottom edge | **3.00 mm** | the supply rail, ~1 A with margin |
| `/DRAIN` runs to J3 and D1 | **1.50 mm** | 300 mA of fan current plus the switching edges |
| minimum drill | **0.80 mm** | standard hobby bit |
| vias | **none** | single-layer board |

The board is planar: `build_board.py` checks every net pair for crossings and
reports `no net crossings - the board is planar, no jumpers needed`. So the 0 Ω
jumper trick was never needed. If you move parts and a crossing appears, the
script prints it and you add a 0 Ω resistor to `JUMPERS` (it is deliberately not
in the schematic).

## Where things sit

Board origin is the bottom-left corner of the outline, X right, **Y down** (KiCad's
board convention). Four 3.2 mm mounting holes at the corners are what set the board
size: without them this circuit fits in roughly 50 × 40 mm.

```
      x=0                                                     x=56
 y=3  ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════   /GND bus, 2.0 mm wide
      R2  R4  R5        MK2                              MK1
                       Q2  →  Q3  →  Q1   →  J3 (3 fans)
 y=41 ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════   /P12V bus, 3.0 mm wide
      J1 (+12 V in)   C3 C4  R3   D1                    MK3  MK4
```

A 1.5 mm `/GND` return lane runs down the left edge (x = 5.5) from the top bus to
J1, C3 and C4, so no current has to travel the whole length of the board twice.

## Pad → net table

**This is the table to build from.** Pad numbers are the **real package pin
numbers**. The schematic symbols are a different story, see the warning below.

### Connectors

| ref | pad | net | board position (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 12 V in | 1 | `/P12V` | (9.00, 37.00) |
| | 2 | `/GND` | (6.46, 37.00) |
| J2 PWM in | 1 | `/PWM` | (11.00, 20.00) |
| | 2 | `/GND` | (8.46, 20.00) |
| J3 fan 1 | 1 / 2 | `/P12V` / `/DRAIN` | (48.54, 31.08) / (46.00, 31.08) |
| J3 fan 2 | 3 / 4 | `/P12V` / `/DRAIN` | (48.54, 28.54) / (46.00, 28.54) |
| J3 fan 3 | 5 / 6 | `/P12V` / `/DRAIN` | (48.54, 26.00) / (46.00, 26.00) |

Each **row** of J3 is one fan: odd pin = +12 V, even pin = the switched side. A real
12 V fan goes between +12 V and `/DRAIN`; the fan's own wire pair plugs straight into
one row. (`/FAN_W` in the schematic is inside the fan *model* — it never appears on
the board.)

| ref | part | package | pin 1 | pin 2 | pin 3 | pins 4–8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Q3 | IRF7416 | SO-8 | `/P12V` (source) | `/P12V` (source) | `/P12V` (source) | 4 = `/BOOT` (gate), 5–8 = `/HIGATE` (drain) |
| Q1 | IRFZ44N | TO-220 | `/GATE` (gate) | `/DRAIN` (drain) | `/GND` (source) | — |
| D1 | 1N5819 | DO-41 | `/P12V` (cathode, banded end) | `/DRAIN` (anode) | — | — |

> **Warning — symbol pins are not package pins.** The SPICE symbols on the sheet
> (`Simulation_SPICE:*`, `Device:*MOS*`) number their pins **D-G-S(-B)**, because
> that is the order ngspice wants for a MOSFET card. The physical devices are not
> wired that way:
>
> | part | SPICE symbol order | real package order |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2N7002 SOT-23 | 1 drain, 2 gate, 3 source | **1 gate, 2 source, 3 drain** |
> | IRF7416 SO-8 | 1 drain, 2 gate, 3 source, 4 bulk | **1–3 source, 4 gate, 5–8 drain** |
> | IRFZ44N TO-220 | 1 drain, 2 gate, 3 source | **1 gate, 2 drain, 3 source** |
>
> The board above is wired to the **package**. If you rebuild the layout by hand,
> follow this table and not the numbers printed next to the transistor symbols.
> KiCad does not warn you about this: it only checks that the *net names* land on
> the pads it was told about.

## Parts on the board vs parts on the sheet

The board carries 14 electrical parts plus 4 mounting holes:

```
J1 J2 J3  R1 R2 R3 R4 R5  Q1 Q2 Q3  D1  C3 C4  MK1 MK2 MK3 MK4
```

These sheet items are **DNP** (do-not-populate) and have no footprint on the board —
they exist only so ngspice can see the real capacitances and the load:

| ref | value | what it really is |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | 1.5 n | the IRFZ44N's own input capacitance (Ciss) |

## Drilling

33 holes, four sizes:

| drill | count | what |
|---|---|---|
| 0.80 mm | 14 | the five resistors (10), C3 (2), C4 (2) |
| 1.00 mm | 10 | the three pin headers (J1 2, J2 2, J3 6) |
| 1.10 mm | 5 | Q1 TO-220 (3) and D1 DO-41 (2) |
| 3.20 mm | 4 | mounting holes (M3) — non-plated |

Drill the small holes with a 0.8 mm bit and open the header holes with a 1.0 mm bit;
the 3.2 mm holes are easiest with a step bit. `pcb/drill/mosfet-fan-switch-drl_map.pdf`
is the map to check against.

## Etching this board

1. Print `docs/pcb-etch-bottom.svg` (or the PDF) at **100 % — do not scale to fit**.
   The SVG's page is exactly the board size, which makes the check trivial.
2. The artwork is already **mirrored**: it is the bottom (copper) side as seen from
   the top. Put the printed side **down** onto the copper, exactly as you would for
   toner transfer. If you print the PDF on A4, line the outline up with the board
   edge before you cut.
3. Black areas are copper that stays. Everything white gets etched away.
4. Cut the outline from `Edge.Cuts` (the rounded rectangle in the plot). The four
   corner holes are on the outline's radius, so drill them before or after etching —
   not through the toner.
5. Drill, then tin the pads. The solder mask layer (`B_Mask.gbs`) is exported only so
   a fab would have it; for a home board it is the drill-and-tin guide.

Silkscreen: all the labels live on **F.SilkS**, the component side. Since there is no
copper on that side the labels never sit on copper, and they read normally when the
board is face-up. `B.SilkS` carries only the two SMD reference labels. If you print
only the copper for transfer, the labels are still useful as a separate assembly
sheet (`docs/pcb-assembly-top.pdf`).

## Assembly order

1. **Q2 (SOT-23) and Q3 (SO-8)** — solder and inspect first; everything else depends
   on them. Check Q3 with a multimeter before continuing: pads 1–3 (source) must be
   continuous with the +12 V bus, pad 4 must be isolated from it.
2. **R1–R5** — values matter here: R2 and R5 are what keep the fans from drifting on
   at power-up.
3. **D1** — the banded end goes to the +12 V side. Backwards, it is a dead short
   across the supply through the FET's body diode.
4. **C4** — `+` to pad 1 (`/P12V`). C3 is not polarised.
5. **Q1 (TO-220)** — the tab is the **drain** on this part as mounted, and the tab is
   live at 12 V switching. Keep it clear of anything on the top side; if you bolt it
   to a heatsink, the heatsink is live too.
6. **J1, J2, J3** last, so the board lies flat while you solder.
7. Mount, then wire: 12 V brick → J1, Arduino pin (D9 / OC1A) → J2, three fans → J3.
   The Arduino stays off the board; J2 is the only connection to it, and J2's pad 2 is
   the shared ground — the Arduino and the 12 V supply must share it.

## KiCad 10 behaviour that shaped this generator

Kept here because it cost real time to find, and it will bite the next board:

- **A footprint's rotation rotates pad *positions* only; a pad's copper rotates only
  by that pad's own `(at x y angle)`.** KiCad itself bakes the placement angle into
  every pad when it places a footprint (a fresh SO-8 at `(at 10 10 90)` is saved with
  pads written `(at -2.575 -1.905 90)`). A hand-written footprint with pad angles left
  at 0 gives you an SO-8 whose pads are still 1.75 × 0.6 mm in an unrotated row —
  overlapping copper, and DRC shorts that look like routing mistakes.
  `build_board.py` bakes the angle: `la = (la - c.a) % 360`.
- **Track end caps are round.** A 2.0 mm track reaching a point bulges 1.0 mm past it.
  That is how a +12 V bus running along an SO-8's pad row shorted into the gate pad
  1.27 mm away. Keep wide runs off dense SMD rows.
- **Text angles in the file are absolute (board-relative)**, not relative to the
  footprint. Emitting `(at x y 0)` gives a label that is horizontal on the board no
  matter how the footprint is rotated; emitting the footprint's own angle gives you a
  label sideways. Board `gr_text` is also **centred** on its position, so a note
  placed at x=29 really starts at x≈25.
- **KiCad mirrors a B.Cu footprint by negating the pad's local Y** — the emitter does
  the same, and writes the footprint angle as `-c.a` because KiCad's rotation
  handedness is the negative of the script's maths convention.
- Footprint library texts smaller than 0.8 mm are skipped by the generator: they are
  too small to etch, and they were the source of the `text_thickness` / `text_height`
  violations.
- The board's footprints reuse the **schematic symbols' UUIDs**, so cross-probing
  between the sheet and the board works and *Update PCB from Schematic* recognises
  the parts instead of treating the board as 18 unknown footprints. The four mounting
  holes have their own UUIDs; they have no symbol, which is normal.

## Known limits of this layout

- It is a **learning board**, not a fab submission: 0.5 mm clearance and 0.8 mm tracks
  are generous for a modern fab and tight for a home etch. It is a good middle ground
  for both.
- No ground plane and no ground pours — the GND is a 3.0 mm bus along the top edge
  plus a return lane down the left edge. For three 100 mA fans at 1 kHz that is fine;
  it is not what you would do for a 10 A output.
- Q1's TO-220 tab is the drain and is switching. Leave clearance around it if you
  ever route on the top side.
- The fan connector is a 2×03 header, not a screw terminal — that is what "use generic
  pin headers for the fan and 12 V connections" bought. If the fans will be tugged on,
  use a screw terminal or lock the cables to the enclosure instead.
