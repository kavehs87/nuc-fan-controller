Parts collection sheet — what is built, and where every part came from

Two halves, and they are not equally live. Section 1 is the perfboard build in fan-switch-booster/ — the circuit that exists, was scoped, and produced every fan-side number in this repo. Sections 2–3 are the cabinet walk for the retired nuc-fan-controller enclosure board: kept because those parts are still in the drawers and this sheet is the record of where they came from, but that board lives at the tag nuc-fan-controller-full-2026-10-23 and is not being built. Section 4 is what the photos and caliper readings have to answer — each item now says whether it still matters. Quantities include spares. Nothing here needs buying except possibly M3 hardware.

4. What the photos and calipers have to answer

Each item now says whether it still matters. Still live applies to any rebuild, including the perfboard; retired board's footprint detail is kept only because the footprints at the tag were drawn from those numbers.

4.1 The resistors — the biggest lever on area

Still live. The design assumes DIN0204: 3.6 mm body, 1.6 mm Ø, 5.08 mm pad pitch. If the cabinet's are 0207 (≈5.5 × 2.5 mm, leads spread to ≈10 mm) every resistor takes twice the room, and a 0207 can also be seated with its body off the board to halve that. Report body length, body Ø, and the spacing the leads land at when the part sits flat. This is also what decides how R1–R5 sit on a perfboard.

4.2 The disc caps

Retired board's footprint detail. The board draws the 100 n as Ø6.0 / W2.5 / P5.00 with its pads spread to 5.08, and the 100 p as Ø3.8 / W2.6 / P2.50. Report each body’s Ø, thickness, and lead spread where the leads exit the body; a smaller disc simply sits loose in the pads, a bigger one is what moves the board.

4.3 Headers — the cabinet has no 4‑pin strip

Still live — any build that plugs into the NUC needs one. Three ways to make J2 and J6: snap 5‑pin strips down to 4; salvage the NUC's own fan plug and solder its wires into a 4‑pin header; or solder fan wires straight into the pads. The JST‑XH 4P housings in L1 E9 are 2.5 mm pitch and will not sit on 2.54 mm pins. Report whether the strips are breakaway.

4.4 The flyback diode's span

Retired board's footprint detail. The board keeps the library’s 10.16 mm pitch so the silkscreen outline matches the part. A DO‑41 body is ≈5.3 × 2.7 mm with ≈2.7 mm natural lead spacing, so its leads get bent out to 10.16 and it lies flat with its body between the two rails it joins. Report body length and Ø. On the perfboard the same part just needs its leads long enough to reach the two rails.

4.5 The buck module

Live if a 12 V rail is ever built as a module. Some XL4016 modules have screw terminals (5.08 mm pitch, no pins at all), some have plain pins. Report module L × W, what the input and output terminals are and their spacing, where the two trim pots sit, whether it has mounting holes, and how tall its tallest part is. The perfboard bench uses an 8 A brick instead, so nothing here blocks it.

4.6 The regulator

Retired board's footprint detail. Confirm the AMS1117‑3.3 is TO‑252 (SOT‑223): the board’s pads are TO‑252, and a SOT‑223 is the same 6.5 mm body with a longer lead stub that will not sit down on them. If only a TO‑220 turns up, L1 B1 also holds BA033ST (TO‑220 3.3 V, 4 pcs) — a deliberate all‑THT option, at the cost of a TO‑220 footprint on a board that is trying to be small.

4.7 The transistors

Still live, and this is the one that breaks hardware silently. TO‑92 leads come flat (one row) or staggered (middle lead offset); the design uses TO-92_Inline_Wide. Report which the BC547s are and confirm the marking reads BC547B. Then check the pin order: these footprints assume the NXP/Philips E‑B‑C order facing the flat face (1 = E, 2 = B, 3 = C), and an onsemi part in C‑B‑E looks identical and works not at all. Meter both before they go in.

4.8 The Pro Mini

Still live. Confirm 3.3 V / 8 MHz (crystal marked 8.000): the 3.3 V rail and the firmware expect it, and a 5 V / 16 MHz board back‑feeds 5 V into every pin. Then measure where its pins actually are. The retired board plugged it into one 1×05 header, which is only right if GND, VCC, A0, D11 and D9 are reachable on one row of the Pro Mini's own headers. Photograph both rows with the pin names legible: if they are split, J5 becomes two headers and the bottom edge of the layout moves.

5. Not in the cabinet

M3 mounting hardware for the board corners (four holes, if they stay) plus four nuts, and hookup wire. The 19 V lead, its fuse, and the NUC's 5 V tap are bench items, not board items.
