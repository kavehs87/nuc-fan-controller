nuc-fan-controller — Mode B

12 V for three 2‑pin 12 V fans, from an XL4016E1 step‑down module bolted to the enclosure and wired to this board through J6; 12 V → 5 V → 3.3 V for a 3.3 V / 8 MHz Arduino Pro Mini; follows the PWM duty the NUC asks for; and simulates the tach signal the ITE expects. Schematic

Drawn with wires, not net labels. Every signal net carries a name tag, so the print, the netlist and the board agree on the names. J2 pin 4 carries a no‑connect flag: that is the NUC header's own fan‑supply wire, which this board never touches.

Board, 124 × 85 mm, two layers

GND poured on both layers with 322 stitching vias. Power nets 1.5 mm, signals 0.2 mm. Flow left to right: the 19 V entry (J1) and the module header (J6), then the 12 V rail to the LDOs and the Pro Mini header; the NUC interface along the top; the fan drive and the three fan connectors down the right edge. The silkscreen between Q1 and Q2 reads Q1 Q2 1=E 2=B 3=C.

Which way round a BC547 goes. The part number alone does not say: NXP/Philips — the order most unbranded TO‑92s use — is 1 emitter · 2 base · 3 collector, while onsemi/Fairchild publish the same number as 1 collector · 2 base · 3 emitter. This board is drawn and routed for E‑B‑C: pad 1, the square pad, is the emitter sitting in the GND pour, and pad 3 carries NUC_TACH on Q1 and FAN_GATE on Q2. Meter them before fitting: read hFE, swap the outer two pins, read again — a BC547B gives 200–450 forward and single digits reversed, so the orientation that reads a real hFE is the one this board wants. BUILD.md carries the full note.

Plus four MountingHole:MountingHole_3.2mm_M3 holes, which are board‑only and therefore correctly absent from the table above. And the converter itself: an XL4016E1 step‑down module (cabinet L1 A10 — 5–40 V in, 1.2–36 V out, 8 A, ~180 kHz, 61 × 41 × 27 mm with its two heatsinks), bolted to the enclosure wall and joined to J6 by four wires. It is a purchased part, so it is not in the table either.

Build order, in short

  1. Identify the four NUC fan‑header wires with a meter. Mark them before cutting anything.
  2. Fit J1, J6, C3 and C4. Set the module to 12.0 V with a meter before it is wired in, and confirm its current limit is released by watching the rail through a fan starting.
  3. Fit U2/U3, confirm 5 V and 3.3 V. Then meter Q1 and Q2 against the pinning note above and fit Q1/Q2/Q3.
  4. Find the fan's stall duty and measure its RPM at two duties. Those four numbers are the calibration constants at the top of the sketch.
  5. Read TP1 with a frequency counter: it must equal rpm / 30 Hz exactly — it is a hardware timer.
  6. Connect the tach side to the NUC alone (J2.1 + J2.2) and read the RPM in BIOS.
  7. Add the PWM wire (J2.3) and watch the serial line while the machine is loaded.
  8. Only then connect the fans to J7/J3/J4 and splice the stock fan cable.
Three things that break hardware: a 12 V fan's tach or PWM wire spliced into the NUC header (that fan pulls those lines up to its own 12 V internally); a 5 V FTDI on a 3.3 V Pro Mini (the ATmega328P's absolute maximum on any pin is VCC + 0.5 V); and a BC547 fitted in the onsemi C-B-E order into a board routed for E-B-C. Flash with Board → Arduino Pro or Pro Mini, Processor → ATmega328 (3.3V, 8MHz).
