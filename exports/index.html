nuc-fan-controller — Mode B

12 V for three 2‑pin 12 V fans, from an XL4016E1 step‑down module bolted to the enclosure and wired to this board through J6; 12 V → 3.3 V for a 3.3 V / 8 MHz Arduino Pro Mini; follows the PWM duty the NUC asks for; and simulates the tach signal the ITE expects. 88.90 × 76.20 mm, one copper layer, made to be etched at home. Schematic

Drawn with wires, not net labels. Every signal net carries a name tag, so the print, the netlist and the board agree on the names. J2 pin 4 carries a no‑connect flag: that is the NUC header's own fan‑supply wire, which this board never touches. The buck is not on the schematic — the module is a purchased part wired to J6 — and neither is R11, the 0 Ω bridge that exists only on the board.

Board — 88.90 × 76.20 mm, one copper layer

F.Cu is the only copper layer and it is the component side: pads, traces, the ground pour and every part are on the same side, so the copper you etch is the copper you solder to. GND is a pour over the whole board — 5 009 mm², one island, solid connection to every GND pad. Flow: 19 V in at J1 top‑right and the module header J6 beside it; the +12 V rail across at y = 17.78 with a riser up the right edge to the 3.3 V regulator; the fan rail (FAN_SW) at y = 20.32 under the three fan connectors; the fan drive in the band at y = 25–43; the tach front end and the PWM divider at y = 50–64; the NUC's fan header J2 bottom‑left and the Pro Mini's header J5 along the bottom edge.

One crossing, bridged by a 0 Ω resistor. The Pro Mini's A0 (PWM_IN) and D11 (FAN_PWM) sit side by side on J5, and they have to go to opposite sides of the divider's row. On one layer there is nothing to route around that, so R11 — a 0 Ω, a scraped‑off lead, or a staple of bare wire — carries FAN_PWM over the divider row at (53.34, 48.25). R11 is a board part only: the schematic has no such component. KiCad's board‑only ratsnest therefore reports one "missing connection" between the two halves of FAN_PWM, which is the bridge doing its job.

Board‑only parts, which KiCad's BOM correctly leaves out: R11, the 0 Ω bridge (same DIN0204 footprint — a 0 Ω, a scraped‑off lead, or a staple of bare wire), and four MountingHole:MountingHole_3.2mm_M3 holes. Purchased parts, also correctly out: the XL4016E1 step‑down module (cabinet L1 A10 — 5–40 V in, 1.2–36 V out, 8 A, ~180 kHz, 61 × 41 × 27 mm with its two heatsinks), bolted to the enclosure wall and joined to J6 by four wires; and the Arduino Pro Mini 3.3 V / 8 MHz that plugs into J5.

Which way round a BC547 goes. The part number alone does not say: NXP/Philips — the order most unbranded TO‑92s use — is 1 emitter · 2 base · 3 collector, while onsemi/Fairchild publish the same number as 1 collector · 2 base · 3 emitter. This board is drawn and laid out for E‑B‑C: pad 1, the square pad, is the emitter sitting in the GND pour, and pad 3 carries NUC_TACH on Q1 and FAN_GATE on Q2. Meter them before fitting: read hFE, swap the outer two pins, read again — a BC547B gives 200–450 forward and single digits reversed, so the orientation that reads a real hFE is the one this board wants. BUILD.md carries the full note.

Build order, in short

  1. Identify the four NUC fan‑header wires with a meter. Mark them before cutting anything.
  2. Cut, etch and drill the board; check every net with a meter against the schematic before anything is soldered.
  3. Solder U3 (tin one pad, seat it, solder the tab, then the rest), then the headers, then the resistors, then the capacitors with the electrolytics' polarity last.
  4. Fit J1, J6, C3 and C4. Set the module to 12.0 V with a meter before it is wired in, and confirm its current limit is released by watching the rail through a fan starting. Then confirm 3.3 V at U3's output.
  5. Find the fan's stall duty and measure its RPM at two duties. Those four numbers are the calibration constants at the top of the sketch.
  6. Read TP1 with a frequency counter: it must equal rpm / 30 Hz exactly — it is a hardware timer.
  7. Connect the tach side to the NUC alone (J2.1 + J2.2) and read the RPM in BIOS.
  8. Add the PWM wire (J2.3) and watch the serial line while the machine is loaded.
  9. Only then connect the fans to J7/J3/J4 and splice the stock fan cable.
Three things that break hardware: a 12 V fan's tach or PWM wire spliced into the NUC header (that fan pulls those lines up to its own 12 V internally); a 5 V FTDI on a 3.3 V Pro Mini (the ATmega328P's absolute maximum on any pin is VCC + 0.5 V); and a BC547 fitted in the onsemi C-B-E order into a board laid out for E-B-C. Flash with Board → Arduino Pro or Pro Mini, Processor → ATmega328 (3.3V, 8MHz).
